Collaborators

Almost nothing we do works without an experimental group on the other side of it. These are the collaborations that shape most of our work.

Shahin Rafii

Weill Cornell Medicine

Our longest-running collaboration, on endothelial heterogeneity, angiocrine signalling and the vascular niche. Joint work runs from the conversion of adult endothelium into haematopoietic stem cells to adaptable endothelial cells for organ regeneration and islet engraftment.

Robert Schwartz

Weill Cornell Medicine

Rob's lab builds stem cell-derived and bioengineered human liver systems. Joint work runs from the pluripotent stem cell organoid platforms used to identify SARS-CoV-2 therapeutics in 2020 through to peribiliary mesenchymal identity in liver homeostasis and injury.

Stacey Huppert

Cincinnati Children's Hospital Medical Center

Liver development and biliary architecture. The Huppert lab works on how hepatocyte plasticity can rebuild insufficient intrahepatic bile duct systems; we work together on the single-cell landscape underlying that process.

Louis Cohen

Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai

Host–microbial interactions and Crohn's disease. Joint work on the mucosal immune and endothelial landscape of the inflamed intestine, and how vascular signals shape the immune response there.

We are recruiting

The lab is currently hiring postdoctoral researchers and research technicians.

Postdocs. We are looking for people who want to work at the interface of computation and vascular or immune biology. Strong candidates might come from a computational background (bioinformatics, machine learning, statistics, physics) and want to engage seriously with the biology, or from an experimental background and want to build real computational depth. Experience with single-cell or epigenomic data is welcome but not required if the quantitative foundation is there.

Technicians. We are looking for organised, careful people to support data generation and analysis workflows. This is a good position for someone planning to apply to graduate or medical school who wants substantive research experience and the chance to be an author on real papers.

To apply, email dar2042@med.cornell.edu with a CV and a short note about what you want to work on and why. Please say something specific. A paper of ours you have opinions about is a much better opening than a generic cover letter.
