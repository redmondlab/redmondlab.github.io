Redmond Lab

David Redmond, PhD · Assistant Professor of Computational Biology Research in Medicine

We study how vascular and immune cells acquire, maintain and lose their identity. Our work combines single-cell transcriptomics, epigenomics and machine learning to read cell state at scale — and to ask what that state means for tissue function in development, ageing and disease.

Research themes Publications

Research themes

01

Computational biology

Methods and analysis frameworks for large-scale single-cell and multiomic data: atlas construction, cross-dataset integration, and reproducible pipelines built to survive the transition from exploration to publication.

02

Transcriptomics & epigenomics

Single-cell and single-nucleus RNA-seq, ATAC and multiome assays used together to connect chromatin state to transcriptional programme — and to identify the transcription factors that specify and maintain cell identity.

03

Artificial intelligence

Machine learning and representation learning applied to cellular state: predicting identity and fate from high-dimensional measurements, and using models as hypothesis generators for experiments rather than as endpoints.

04

Vascular–immune interactions

The endothelium is not passive plumbing. We study how blood vessels instruct immune and stem cells through niche signals, and how that dialogue changes in inflammation, infection, ageing and regeneration.
